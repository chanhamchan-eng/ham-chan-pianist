Hong Kong pianist Ham Chan is recognized for the poetic clarity and expressive depth of his artistry. His debut album, Masks under the Mask (Centaur Records, 2025), devoted to Robert Schumann’s piano character pieces, received a Silver Award from the Global Music Awards and has drawn international critical attention. China Art praised the recording’s delicate tone and refined sensitivity, while Fanfare noted Chan’s clarity of articulation and deep respect for the score, concluding that in moments of Eusebius, Chan is among the finest interpreters. Writing in the American Record Guide, critics observed a distinctive artistic flair shaped by an independent musical upbringing and a strong affinity for character-driven repertoire.

Chan has performed across Europe, Asia, and North America, with festival appearances including Stars at the Rhine in Switzerland, Beethoven Bootcamp in Ireland, the Rebecca Penneys Piano Festival in the United States, and Music Fest Perugia in Italy. His performances and recordings have also reached audiences through radio broadcasts, including Alabama Public Radio and WCNY. Alongside his performance career, he is active as an educator and has presented a masterclass at Salisbury University.

Known for his imaginative approach to programming, Chan is drawn to projects that connect music with literary, historical, and psychological ideas. His repertoire spans from Bach to contemporary works, with particular attention to music that explores narrative, contrast, and inner character. This curiosity also informs his work as Founder and Artistic Director of the Capital Keys Performing Arts Foundation and Capital Keys Concert Series, which he established not only to broaden public access to classical music, but also as a platform for exploring new possibilities in concert programming—bringing together distinctive repertoire, thematic ideas, collaborations, and educational perspectives to create more engaging ways of experiencing classical music.

Among these initiatives is Schubert 200, a nine-concert exploration of Schubert’s chamber music with piano and song leading to the composer’s bicentenary in 2028. Developed across multiple seasons, the project approaches Schubert’s music as an evolving artistic journey rather than a conventional concert cycle, bringing together performers, repertoire, and contextual programming to illuminate different dimensions of the composer’s musical world.

Chan began formal piano study at the age of eighteen, a late start that fostered an independent and introspective artistic voice. He holds a Doctor of Musical Arts in Piano Performance from the University of Maryland, College Park, and studied previously at the Mannes School of Music, the Boston Conservatory at Berklee, and Hong Kong Baptist University. His teachers include Larissa Dedova, Vladimir Valjarevic, Janice Weber, Gregory Haimovsky, and Pang Jane Cheung.
